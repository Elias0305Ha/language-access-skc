# Correspondence with King County

This file records the exchange that followed sending `docs/MEMO.md`. It is
published because the correction it contains matters more than the original
claim did.

---

## 1. Memo sent

**Date:** September 2026
**To:** King County Language Access Program, `kcla@kingcounty.gov`
**From:** Elias Hakenso

Full text in `docs/MEMO.md`. The central claim was that King County's
language tier map, the benchmark named in KCC 2.15.030.B, rests on 2016
Census data and omits Dari (3,040 families), Pashto (1,127) and Marshallese
(446). The ask was a single data refresh.

---

## 2. Reply from King County

**Date:** October 2026
**From:** Cheryse Ishii, Language Access Manager, King County Language Access

Quoted with the key passages intact:

> King County's language tiers were recently updated, and both Dari and
> Pashto are reflected among our current priority languages based on more
> recent 2023-2024 data. Spanish remains Tier 1, with Dari and Pashto
> included in Tier 2.
>
> We generally update the tiers every two years using several demographic and
> service-level data sources. Because Census data carry some inherent lag, we
> generally rely on the more stable five-year estimates, particularly for
> smaller populations. We supplement those data with service-level
> information from County systems as well, including our public health
> clinics and court systems.
>
> The tier map you located reflects an earlier version of the County's
> language tiers. The maps and tiers included as appendices to the Executive
> Order reflect the information in place at the time the policy was issued
> and remain part of that historical policy document.
>
> We are working with our departments and IT partners to centralize the
> management and publication of these resources so that the current versions
> are easier to identify and access. Our most recent tier information and
> maps were moved to our employee intranet as part of broader web-content
> changes that shifted internal operational resources off the public site. We
> have continued to receive inquiries from community members and partners
> looking for this information, however, and are working to make the current
> tiers and maps publicly available again.

---

## 3. What this changes

**The central claim in the memo was wrong.** Dari and Pashto are on the
current tiers. The County re-ranks every two years and used 2023-2024 data.
That is a better process than the memo assumed, and the correction is
accepted without qualification.

**What survives is narrower and, in one respect, sharper.**

The 2018 appendix was the only version of the tier map locatable in several
weeks of searching. The County confirms the current version sits on an
internal intranet and that other community members and partners have hit the
same wall.

KCC 2.15.030.B makes the tier map the legal benchmark for which documents
must be translated. While that list is not published, no resident, school
district or community organization can check whether the ordinance is being
met. Compliance becomes unverifiable from outside the County.

The County states it is working to republish. That is the right fix and this
project's remaining ask is simply that it happen.

**What is unaffected.** None of the following depends on the tier map:

- 51-agency, 7-sector supply inventory, 6,272 scored rows
- 393 families with no written provision in any sector
- 71 of 77 sampled locale page pairs identical to the English page
- King County's Amharic pages identified as machine translation
- 95 geocoded service points; Dari served at 2, Pashto, Tigrinya and Khmer at 0
- The verification problem: 155 tier-3 claims, 2 read by a speaker of the
  language, 2 of the 4 checked found to be machine output

---

## 4. Open questions put back to the County

1. A copy of the current tier list, since it is not publicly available.
2. Whether **Marshallese** is on the current tiers. The reply named Dari and
   Pashto and did not address it. Marshallese is 446 families in the study
   area and the most geographically concentrated community in the dataset,
   heavily concentrated in Auburn.
3. An offer to test the republished resource for findability once it is live.

---

## 5. Note on method

This correction is left in the repository rather than edited away. The
original claim, the primary-source rebuttal, and the revised finding are all
recorded. An analysis that was never challenged is not evidence of
correctness, and an analysis that quietly absorbs a correction is worse than
one that prints it.
